Build your plan

  1. Pick your degree plan from the Degree menu.
  2. Turn on Completed for every course you have already finished.
  3. Turn on Enrolling for the courses you are taking in the selected term.
  4. Move to the next term with the gold arrow, or jump to any term from the Term menu.
  5. Repeat steps 3 and 4 until you reach graduation.

Your choices build up the course plan at the bottom of the page. Print gives you just that plan and your notes, ready for an advising appointment.

The wizard covers courses for a major or minor in Physics and Astronomy. It does not yet track liberal education requirements, it knows nothing about your second major or minor, and it cannot handle repeatable variable-credit courses such as PHYS 399 or PHYS 495.

This tool is not a substitute for meeting with your advisors. Meet with your academic and faculty advisors each semester to confirm your plan and to talk through research, internships, careers, and graduate study.

Reading the board

  • Available You can take it in the selected term.
  • Enrolling You have chosen it for the selected term.
  • Completed Finished in this term or an earlier one.
  • Unavailable Shaded. Not offered this term, or you are missing a requirement.

A course with a filled header is one you have to take to finish the selected plan. That covers the plan's outright requirements, any option you have not ruled out yet, and anything those courses depend on — which is why the calculus sequence is filled even though it earns no credit toward the major. Applied Physics majors, for instance, must take PHYS 255 and PHYS 356, or either PHYS 365 or PHYS 375 — once any one of those is satisfied, the others go quiet. You have met the major requirements when every filled-header course is completed and you have the credits counted toward the major.

An unavailable course may be waiting on a corequisite, a prerequisite, a more complex set of courses, or the term it is offered in. Open Details on any card to see what it needs. PHYS 486 has a looser requirement than the wizard can model, so it may look available early.
